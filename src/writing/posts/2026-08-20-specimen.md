<p class="dropcap">The purpose of a specimen is to show the type doing its
actual job rather than sitting in a grid. Long-form reading is where a serif
either holds up or falls apart, and it falls apart in the places you cannot
see in a heading: the second line of a paragraph, the gap after a comma, the
way numerals sit next to words. Newsreader carries an optical-size axis, so
at reading size the strokes thicken slightly and the counters open up.</p>

Running text continues here so there is enough of it to judge rhythm. A
measure of roughly sixty-five characters keeps the eye from losing its place
on the return sweep, and the line height is set against the x-height rather
than picked from a round number.

## A second-level heading

Headings come from the same scale as everything else. The gap between a
heading and its paragraph is one step of the spacing scale, and the gap above
it is three, so the heading belongs to what follows rather than floating
between two blocks.

<aside class="marginnote"><p>Margin notes sit beside the text on wide screens
and fold back into the flow below 60rem, where there is no margin to sit in.
They are ordinary asides in the source order, so a screen reader meets them
where they belong.</p></aside>

Here is a paragraph that a margin note is attached to. On a wide screen the
note sits to the right of this column; on a narrow one it appears directly
underneath, indented and ruled.

<blockquote class="pullquote">
  <p>A number without its reasoning attached does not survive an argument.</p>
</blockquote>

The pull quote above is deliberately not centred and deliberately not in
quotation marks. It is set larger, in the display face, and hangs into the
margin on wide screens.
